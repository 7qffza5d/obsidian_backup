# Anatomy of a Linguistics Olympiad problem

A linguistics olympiad problem is more like a logic puzzle. You are given some clues (a corpus) and tasked to infer linguistic rules form these clues. I think a markdown of the anatomy of these clues could very well prove helpful. 

### Part One: Intro
I call this the intro, but sometimes they are at the end of the corpus, or even the end of the questions. The intro consists of several things. Maybe it clarifies what class of linguistic structure you are looking at (sentences, phrases, words, something bigger or smaller?). Maybe it describes the special diacritics used for tones in this transcription system. Maybe it tells the language family this language comes from, and where it's used. Maybe it assigns meanings to the IPA consonant symbols which hopefully eventually just bleeds away for me and I recognize them in a heartbeat. That's the gist of the intro. 

### Part Two: Corpus
The corpus, or dataset, is a set of linguistic structures given in the questioned language, and their translations. If the structure matches the translation, as in you know which translation belongs to which structure, then that is called a *Rosetta Stone* problem, otherwise it is a *chaos-and-order* problem. Sometimes, a structure is given without a translation, or a translation is given without the structure. They will become the tasks you will have to answer, but I chose to include them here because they can provide you with more clues.

### Part Three: Tasks
Finally, the tasks. These are things the questions task you with answering. Shocking, I know. Maybe they ask you to provide the translation or structure omitted in the corpus, as I described above. Maybe they ask you to provide the translation or structure to something new. Maybe they ask you something about the language's rules. The tasks are devised such that you can answer the questions purely on the corpus given, so you can assume the info you are given will be enough, which can be a useful clue in some cases.
