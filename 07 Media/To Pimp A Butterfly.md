# To Pimp A Butterfly

## First impression

I roughly remember the first time I listened to TPAB. It was night/early morning in July, I was doing some Physics lab report when I decided to listen to this. Instantly fell in love. I listened to it a shitton of times over the course of the next six months or so. Great album, fantastic album, one of the greatest albums ever, literally changed my life.
## What stayed with me

Broadly, the rap/jazz fusion is extremely addictive. The production was otherworldly. I think I cried the first time I listened to u and i. Maybe The Blacker The Berry radicalized me. I was listening to Alright pretty much every morning. As I listened to it more, my thoughts developed. The renewal of the self in Wesley's Theory, the feeling of a mundane madness in Institutionalized, the self-hatred in u, the spirit to keep going shown in Alright. Coming home and finding that home never really changed, you did. The rage against pretty much every facet of society in The Blacker The Berry, the self-acceptance in You Ain't Gotta Lie and i, and of course, the entire message of Mortal Man. I do hold that this album I listened to 14 months ago was one of the most defining piece of art of my teenage existence. 
## Synopsis

There is a semi-obvious narrative throughout the album, so I think it would fit to go song by song, summarizing each one's meanings.

Quick background of course. Kendrick Lamar had just blown up after the release of good kid, m.A.A.d city. He is now a superstar, and this album is a navigation of this newfound fame and the things that you leave behind.
